# In Re: Amendments to Florida Probate Rules - 2026 Legislation

Supreme Court of Florida
                            ____________

                         No. SC2026-1351
                           ____________

     IN RE: AMENDMENTS TO FLORIDA PROBATE RULES -
                   2026 LEGISLATION.

                          October 15, 2026

PER CURIAM.

     In response to recent legislation, The Florida Bar’s Probate

Rules Committee has filed a “fast-track” report proposing

amendments to Florida Probate Rules 5.122 (Curators) and 5.905

(Form for Petition; Notice; and Order for Appointment of Guardian

Advocate of the Person). 1 The amendments are based on statutory

changes in chapters 2026-85 and 2026-129, Laws of Florida, which

went into effect on July 1, 2026.

     The Committee voted to approve the amendments by a vote of

25-0-0. After considering the Committee’s proposals and the

relevant legislation, we amend rules 5.122 and 5.905 as proposed

     1. We have jurisdiction. See art. V, § 2(a), Fla. Const.; see
also Fla. R. Gen. Prac. & Jud. Admin. 2.140(e).

by the Committee. The more significant amendments are discussed

below.

     Rule 5.122 is amended to clarify that the amount of bond to

be posted by curators, if any, must be determined by the court.

The amendment further clarifies that in addition to the formal

notice given to the person entitled to letters of administration,

notice of the appointment of a curator must also be given to other

interested persons, except when giving notice to other interested

persons would delay the appointment of the curator and there is a

significant danger that the delay would result in the decedent’s

property being wasted, destroyed, or removed beyond the court’s

jurisdiction. This is consistent with section 733.501, Florida

Statutes, as amended by chapter 2026-85, section 1, Laws of

Florida.

     Next, we amend rule 5.905(a) to add “Tatton-Brown-Rahman

syndrome” to the list of disorders and syndromes that constitute a

developmental disability. This change is consistent with section

393.063(11), Florida Statutes, as amended by chapter 2026-129,

section 1, Laws of Florida.

     Accordingly, the Florida Probate Rules are amended as

reflected in the appendix to this opinion. New language is indicated

by underscoring; deletions are indicated by struck-through type.

The amendments shall take effect immediately upon the release of

this opinion. Because the amendments were not published for

comment prior to their adoption, interested persons have 75 days

from the date of this opinion to file comments with the Court. 2

     It is so ordered.

RULE 5.122.      CURATORS

      (a) Petition for Appointment. The petition for appointment
of a curator must be verified and must contain:

           (1) - (7)   [No Change]

      The court may appoint a curator sua sponte to protect the
interests of the estate or a decedent’s heirs.

     (b) Appointment. Before letters of curatorship are issued,
the curator must file a designation of resident agent and
acceptance, and an oath, as is required for personal representatives
under these rules, and the court must determine the amount of
bond, if any, required. The court must issue letters of curatorship
that state the entitlement of the curator to possess or control all or
part of the decedent’s property, which the court may enforce
through contempt proceedings.

     (c)   Notice.

          (1) Formal notice must be given to the person
apparently entitled to letters, if any.

           (2)   Notice must be given to other interested persons.

           (3) If it is likelyIf there is significant danger that the
decedent’s property will be wasted, destroyed, or removed beyond
the jurisdiction of the court and if the appointment of a curator
would be delayed by giving notice, the court may appoint a curator
with formal notice to the person apparently entitled to letters, but
without notice to other interested persons.
