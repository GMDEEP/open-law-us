# In Re: Amendments to Florida Rule of Civil Procedure 1.200

Supreme Court of Florida
                            ____________

                         No. SC2025-1894
                           ____________

       IN RE: AMENDMENTS TO FLORIDA RULE OF CIVIL
                    PROCEDURE 1.200.

                          October 15, 2026

PER CURIAM.

     The Florida Bar’s Civil Procedure Rules Committee filed a

report proposing amendments to Florida Rule of Civil Procedure

1.200 (Case Management; Pretrial Procedure). 1 The Committee

published the proposed amendments for comment on the Bar’s

website before filing them with the Court. Two comments were

received, and the Committee declined to revise its proposal based

on the comments. The Court republished the proposed

amendments for comment. One comment was received from

attorney Dan Cytryn, and the Committee filed a response to the

comment.

     1. We have jurisdiction. See art. V, § 2(a), Fla. Const.; see
also Fla. R. Gen. Prac. & Jud. Admin. 2.140(b).

     Having considered the Committee’s report, the comment filed,

and the Committee’s response, we decline to amend rule 1.200(h) as

proposed by the Committee. We instead amend the rule to require

courts to consider the “availability of the parties,” along with the

needs of the case and the resources of the court, in setting a new

trial period in a case that does not reach trial during the period set

by court order.

     Accordingly, the Florida Rules of Civil Procedure are amended

as set forth in the appendix to this opinion. New language is

indicated by underscoring; deletions are indicated by struck-

through type. The amendments become effective on January 1,

2027, at 12:01 a.m.

     It is so ordered.

COURIEL, C.J., and LABARGA, MUÑIZ, GROSSHANS, FRANCIS,
BARRIOS, and TANENBAUM, JJ., concur.

                               APPENDIX

RULE 1.200.      CASE MANAGEMENT; PRETRIAL PROCEDURE

     (a) – (g)   [No Change]

     (h)   If Trial Is Not Reached During Trial Period.

            (1) If a trial is not reached during the trial period set by
court order, the court must enter an order setting a new trial period
that is as soon as practicable, given the needs of the case, andthe
resources of the court, and the availability of the parties.

          (2) The order resetting the trial period must reflect
what further activity will or will not be permitted.
