# In Re: Amendments to Florida Rule of Civil Procedure 1.530, Florida Small Claims Rule 7.180, and Florida Family Law Rule of Procedure 12.530

Supreme Court of Florida
                             ____________

                          No. SC2025-1795
                            ____________

     IN RE: AMENDMENTS TO FLORIDA RULE OF CIVIL
PROCEDURE 1.530, FLORIDA SMALL CLAIMS RULE 7.180, AND
   FLORIDA FAMILY LAW RULE OF PROCEDURE 12.530.

                         November 12, 2026

PER CURIAM.

     The Florida Bar’s Civil Procedure Rules Committee, Small

Claims Rules Committee, and Family Law Rules Committee

(collectively, “Committees”) have filed a joint report proposing

amendments to Florida Rule of Civil Procedure 1.530 (Motions for

New Trial and Rehearing; Amendments of Judgments; Remittitur or

Additur), Florida Small Claims Rule 7.180 (Motions for New Trial;

Time for; Contents), and Florida Family Law Rule of Procedure

12.530 (Motions for New Trial and Rehearing; Amendments of

Judgments). 1

      1. We have jurisdiction. Art. V, § 2(a), Fla. Const.; see also
Fla. R. Gen. Prac. & Jud. Admin. 2.140(b)(1).

     We published the proposed amendments for comment and

received a comment from the Family Law Section of The Florida

Bar. The Committees filed a response and an amended appendix,

making a modification based on the Family Law Section’s comment.

Having considered the proposed amendments, the comment

received, and the Committees’ response, we hereby amend rules

1.530, 7.180, and 12.530 as proposed with a few modifications

discussed below. The primary function of the amendments is to

reorganize the subdivisions of each rule so that motions for new

trial and motions for rehearing will be addressed separately, making

the rules easier to follow.

     Regarding rule 1.530, subdivision (a) (Motion for New Trial)

addresses procedures to be followed in actions tried by a jury to

verdict and is comprised of three internal subdivisions: (a)(1) (Time

for Motion); (a)(2) (Time for Serving Affidavits); and (a)(3) (Order

Granting to Specify Grounds). Subdivision (b) (Motion for

Rehearing) covers motions for rehearing and is comprised of three

internal subdivisions: (b)(1) (Time for Motion); (b)(2) (Non-Jury

Actions); and (b)(3) (Necessity of Motion). Subdivision (b) is further

                                  -2-

amended to clarify that motions for rehearing may be directed only

to final judgments or final orders.

     The existing subdivisions of rule 1.530 are relettered to

accommodate the above changes, and one subdivision is retitled.

Specifically, existing subdivision (d) (On Initiative of Court) is now

subdivision (c) and titled “New Trial or Rehearing on Initiative of

Court.” Existing subdivisions (e) (When Motion Is Unnecessary;

Non-Jury Action) and (f) (Order Granting to Specify Grounds) are

deleted because their contents have been incorporated into

amended subdivisions (a) and (b), and subdivisions (g) (Motion to

Alter or Amend a Judgment) and (h) (Motion for Remittitur or

Additur) are relettered as (d) and (e). A Committee Note is added to

explain these changes.

     Similar organizational changes are made to rules 7.180 and

12.530. Rule 7.180 is retitled “Motions for New Trial and

Rehearing; Amendments of Judgments; Time For.” Subdivision (b)

of rule 7.180 now clarifies that motions for rehearing are authorized

under the rule but may be directed only to a final judgment or final

order.

                                  -3-

     We make several modifications to the Committees’ proposal.

First, the Committees propose adding to rule 7.180(a) a sentence

reading: “Failure to file a motion for rehearing does not waive the

right to an appeal.” We decline to add this sentence. We also

replace the word “any” with the word “an” in the phrase “any order”

in subdivision (a)(3) of each rule. We delete the words “in a non-

jury action” from subdivision (b)(1) of each rule. And the Court

adds a new subdivision (b)(3) (Necessity of Motion) to each rule,

adjusting the placement of the relevant sentences accordingly.

Finally, we delete from rule 7.180(a)(1) a clause about non-jury

actions because rule 7.180(a) as amended only applies to an action

tried to verdict by a jury.

     The Court hereby amends the Florida Rules of Civil Procedure,

Florida Small Claims Rules, and Florida Family Law Rules of

Procedure as reflected in the appendix to this opinion. New

language is indicated by underscoring; deletions are indicated by

struck-through type. The amendments become effective January 1,

2027, at 12:01 a.m.

     It is so ordered.

RULE 1.530.      MOTIONS FOR NEW TRIAL AND REHEARING;
                 AMENDMENTS OF JUDGMENTS; REMITTITUR
                 OR ADDITUR

      (a) Jury and Non-Jury ActionsMotion for New Trial. AIn
an action tried by a jury to a verdict, a motion for new trial may be
granted to all or any of the parties and on all or a part of the issues.
To preserve for appeal a challenge to the failure of the trial court to
make required findings of fact in the final judgment, a party must
raise that issue in a motion for rehearing under this rule. On a
motion for a rehearing of matters heard without a jury, including
summary judgments, the court may open the judgment if one has
been entered, take additional testimony, and enter a new judgment.

           (b) Time for Motion. (1)          Time for Motion. A motion
for new trial or for rehearing must be served not later than 15 days
after the return of the verdict in a jury action or the date of filing of
the judgment in a non-jury action. A timely motion may be
amended to state new grounds in the discretion of the court at any
time before the motion is determined.

            (c) Time for Serving Affidavits.(2)       Time for
Serving Affidavits. When a motion for a new trial is based on
affidavits, the affidavits must be served with the motion. The
opposing party has 10 days after such service within which to serve
opposing affidavits, which period may be extended for an additional
period not exceeding 20 days either by the court for good cause
shown or by the parties by written stipulation. The court may
permit reply affidavits.

           (3) Order Granting to Specify Grounds. If the court
grants a new trial, the order must state the specific grounds for
granting the new trial. If an order granting new trial is appealed and
does not state the specific grounds, the appellate court must
relinquish its jurisdiction to the trial court for entry of an order
specifying the grounds for granting the new trial.

                                  -6-

     (b) Motion for Rehearing. A motion for rehearing may be
directed only to a final judgment or final order.

          (1) Time for Motion. A motion for rehearing must be
served not later than 15 days after the date of filing of the final
judgment or final order. A timely motion may be amended to state
new grounds in the discretion of the court at any time before the
motion is determined.

           (2) Non-Jury Actions. On a motion for a rehearing of
matters heard without a jury, including summary judgments, the
court may open the judgment if one has been entered, take
additional testimony, and enter a new judgment.

            (3) Necessity of Motion. To preserve for appeal a
challenge to the failure of the trial court to make required findings
of fact in the final judgment, a party must raise that issue in a
motion for rehearing under this rule. In a non-jury action, the
sufficiency of the evidence to support the judgment may be raised
on appeal whether or not the party raising the question has made
any objection thereto in the trial court or made a motion for
rehearing.

      (dc) New Trial or Rehearing Oon Initiative of Court. Not
later than 15 days after the date of filing of the judgment or within
the time of ruling on a timely motion for a rehearing or a new trial
made by a party, the court of its own initiative may order a
rehearing or a new trial for any reason for which it might have
granted a rehearing or a new trial on motion of a party.

     (e) When Motion Is Unnecessary; Non-Jury Action. In a
non-jury action, the sufficiency of the evidence to support the
judgment may be raised on appeal whether or not the party raising
the question has made any objection thereto in the trial court or
made a motion for rehearing, for new trial, or to alter or amend the
judgment.

     (f)   Order Granting to Specify Grounds. All orders granting
a new trial must state the specific grounds therefor. If such an
order is appealed and does not state the specific grounds, the

entry of an order specifying the grounds for granting the new trial.

      (gd) Motion to Alter or Amend a Judgment. A motion to
alter or amend the judgment shallmust be served not later than 15
days after the date of filing of the judgment, except that this rule
does not affect the remedies in rule 1.540(b).

new trial is filed, the court must initially make a determination if a
hearing on the motion is required. If a hearing is required, the court

then the court must enter an order granting or denying the motion

      (he) Motion to Alter or Amend a Judgment. A motion to
alter or amend the judgment must be served not later than 15 days
after entry of the judgment, except that this rule does not affect the
remedies in rule 12.540(b).
