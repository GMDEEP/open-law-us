# In Re: Amendments to Florida Rules of Appellate Procedure 9.370 and 9.900

Supreme Court of Florida
                            ____________

                         No. SC2026-0041
                           ____________

   IN RE: AMENDMENTS TO FLORIDA RULES OF APPELLATE
              PROCEDURE 9.370 AND 9.900.

                          October 8, 2026

PER CURIAM.

     The Florida Bar’s Appellate Court Rules Committee filed a

report proposing amendments to Florida Rules of Appellate

Procedure 9.370 (Amicus Curiae) and 9.900 (Forms). 1 The Court

published the proposed amendments for comment, but no

comments were received. We adopt the amendments as proposed

by the Committee and discuss the more significant amendments

below.

     First, in rule 9.370, we amend subdivision (b) (Contents and

Form) to clarify that the statement of the identity of the amicus

curiae and its interest in the case is included in the word count.

     1. We have jurisdiction. See art. V, § 2(a), Fla. Const.; see
also Fla. R. Gen. Prac. & Jud. Admin. 2.140(b)(1).

Next, throughout rule 9.900, we amend the forms to include a

certificate of service. Additionally, in subdivision (m) (Notice of

Constitutional Question), we add a certificate of service specifically

for the attorney general, who is required to be served in those cases

raising a constitutional challenge to a state statute or state

constitutional provision under rule 9.425.

     Accordingly, the Florida Rules of Appellate Procedure are

amended as set forth in the appendix to this opinion. New language

is indicated by underscoring; deletions are indicated by struck-

through type. The amendments become effective January 1, 2027,

at 12:01 a.m.

     It is so ordered.

COURIEL, C.J., and LABARGA, MUÑIZ, GROSSHANS, FRANCIS,
BARRIOS, and TANENBAUM, JJ., concur.

                             APPENDIX

RULE 9.370.     AMICUS CURIAE

     (a)   [No Change]

      (b) Contents and Form. An amicus brief must comply with
rule 9.210(b) but shallmust omit a statement of the case and facts
and may not exceed 5,000 words if computer-generated or 20 pages
if handwritten or typewritten. The cover must identify the party or
parties supported. An amicus brief must include a concise
statement of the identity of the amicus curiae and its interest in the
case, which is included in the word count.

     (c)   [No Change]

      (d) Notice of Intent to File Amicus Brief in the Supreme
Court of Florida. When a party has invoked the discretionary
jurisdiction of the supreme court, an amicus curiae may file a
notice with the court indicating its intent to seek leave to file an
amicus brief on the merits should the court accept jurisdiction. The
notice shallmust state briefly why the case is of interest to the
amicus curiae, but shallmust not contain argument. The body of
the notice shallmust not exceed 250 words if computer-generated or
1 page if handwritten or typewritten.
